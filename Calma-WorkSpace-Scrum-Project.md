## Section A. Project Statement

### A.1 Context and Objective

*Calma WorkSpace* is a collective work and study center. It welcomes university students, secondary-school pupils, developers, and entrepreneurs who rent rooms to study, work alone or in groups, hold work meetings, or attend group training sessions. The center is run by a manager, a front-desk/monitoring agent (reception, daily schedule), and a payment agent (collection of payments).

**Your mission:** following the Scrum method, develop a web application for room booking that:

- lets users book a room suited to the nature of the reservation (individual or multi-person; personal study, personal task, meeting, group training);
- calculates and records payment (hourly or daily rate; monthly subscription as an option — optional extension);
- tracks room occupancy.

### A.2 Project Framework

| | |
|---|---|
| **Number of participants** | 3 to 4 students |
| **Project duration** | 6 weeks |
| **Project management tools** | Trello, Jira, or GitHub Projects |
| **UML tools** | StarUML, Enterprise Architect, Modelio |
| **Testing tools** | JUnit, pytest, or Jest |
| **Defense and report submission** | Last session of the integrated course (CI) |

### A.3 Scope and Requirements

**Each group's contribution:** The minimum product to deliver covers the whole booking journey.

- **Increment 1:** account and profile management for the center's different audiences (student, secondary-school pupil, developer, entrepreneur); secure authentication; room configuration by the manager (capacity, type, equipment); and viewing availability by date, time slot, and number of people.
- **Increment 2:** booking a room, alone or as a group depending on the nature of the reservation; preventing overlaps; and enforcing capacity, maximum duration, and the buffer time between two reservations.
- **Increment 3:** calculating the price per hour or per day; letting the payment agent record the payment and issue a receipt; and giving the monitoring agent the daily schedule.

**Optional contributions (with bonus):** Extensions to the final product (monthly subscription, parental authorization for minors, cancellation, notifications, attendance tracking, dashboard) are optional and rewarded.

> **N.B.:** A feature is considered done only if its code has been peer-reviewed, its tests pass, its acceptance criteria are validated, and it is executable in a test environment.

## Section B. Required Work

Each team submits a single PDF report of about **30 to 40 pages**, written by the students  preparing the artifacts** (Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, UML diagrams) and a working version of the app. The submission date is the last session of the integrated course. The report must be structured in 4 chapters.

### Chapter 1 — The Team and Roles

- **Part 1:** presents each member, the role they held in each Sprint (Product Owner, Scrum Master, or developer), and the features and tasks they completed.
- **Part 2:** presents the product vision, the actors, then the final Product Backlog.
- **Part 3:** describes the working agreements and the tools chosen.

### Chapters 2, 3 and 4 — One Chapter per Sprint (or Increment)

Each follows the same four-part plan:

1. **Analysis:** the Sprint goal and the selected User Stories.
2. **Design:** the Sprint's UML diagrams and useful mockups.
3. **Implementation:** the Sprint Backlog (tasks, owners, hours), technical choices, significant code excerpts, and difficulties encountered.
4. **Testing:** unit tests and acceptance tests.

Each chapter ends with the **Sprint Review** and the **burndown chart**.

### Validation by Defense and Evaluation

The work is validated through a defense: a presentation of **at most 10 minutes per group**. Each student's final grade combines five marks:

1. the presentation,
2. the report,
3. the discussion,
4. teamwork,
5. attendance at course sessions.

*End.*
